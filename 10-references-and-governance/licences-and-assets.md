## Redistribution position

**What may be redistributed freely, with the stated notices:**

- The InfraScan application source code, under MIT, with the upstream copyright notice retained.
- The bundled fonts, under the OFL, with their licence files.
- The documentation's own screenshots and evaluation images.

**What must not be redistributed without further permission:**

| Item | Why | Current handling |
|---|---|---|
| `weights/best.pt` | Trained with AGPL-covered tooling; training-data rights unresolved | Excluded; SHA-256 published for identification only |
| The dataset images and labels | Per-source licences and creators unverified | Excluded; documented as unverified |
| Ultralytics-provided pretrained weights | AGPL/Enterprise terms | Never bundled; obtained from Ultralytics at runtime if ever needed |
| The GLBITM templates | Institutional property | Held outside the repository; only the structure is documented |
| The `ultralytics` package source | AGPL | Installed as a dependency, never vendored |

**The practical rule for this project:** *the code travels; the data and the weights do not.* Anyone who needs to reproduce the reported evaluation must supply a compatible checkpoint and the dataset themselves, or accept that the result is not reproducible from the public repository alone. This is stated in the [verified results](../09-testing-and-results/verified-results.md) and the [evidence index](../09-testing-and-results/evidence-index.md), not left for the reader to discover.

## What the repository's MIT licence does and does not cover

**It covers:** the original source files in this repository and in the code repository, including the modifications the team made to the upstream files.

**It does not cover:**

- the dataset or the trained checkpoint, which are not part of the repository;
- third-party dependencies, each of which carries its own licence;
- the bundled fonts, which carry the OFL and are not relicensed by MIT;
- the institutional templates, which are not distributed at all;
- the accuracy claims, which are factual statements about a specific run and are bounded by the evidence in [`../PROJECT_FACTS.md`](../PROJECT_FACTS.md).

This distinction is why the [citation guide](citation-guide.md) asks for the environment alongside every metric: a licence lets someone use the code; the evidence lets them judge the result.

## Before a public release

If the project is ever published, packaged or demonstrated beyond the institution, complete these in order:

1. **Resolve the dataset** — identify each source collection, record its creator, URL and licence, and obtain permission where required. Replace the collection entirely if a source is unusable.
2. **Resolve the checkpoint** — confirm the terms for redistributing a model trained with the current toolchain, or continue to exclude it and publish only the training code plus a reproducibility recipe.
3. **Review the AGPL implications** of the inference and training toolchain for the intended distribution model, with a maintainer who can act on the answer.
4. **Fill the academic placeholders** — group ID, supervisor name and designation, department and session — on every deliverable.
5. **Carry the notices** — the upstream MIT notice, the two font OFLs, and any notices required by the dependencies if a binary artefact is produced.
6. **Keep the templates out** of the distribution, or obtain institutional permission.
7. **Re-run the evaluation** if the model, data or preprocessing changed, and publish the new numbers alongside — never in place of — the current ones.
8. **Attach the evidence** — the evaluation summary, the PR curve and the screenshots — so a reader can check the claims rather than take them on trust.

Steps 1–3 are legal questions, not engineering ones. They need a human decision; no amount of documentation substitutes for them.
